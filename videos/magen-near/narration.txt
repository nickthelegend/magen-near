One NEAR. Signed twice. Magen checks a classical signature and a quantum safe signature before your money moves. Now forge the quantum half. NEAR rejects the transfer. Your funds stay put.

That is Magen. Your NEAR account, with a shield for what comes next. Today's account keys rely on mathematics a sufficiently powerful quantum computer could break. Those computers do not exist today. But waiting until they do would be the wrong time to start protecting an account.

NEAR now gives developers two powerful building blocks. A native post quantum access key, and a host function that verifies ML DSA signatures inside a smart contract. Magen brings them into a wallet you can actually use.

Start with a fresh account. Your browser creates two independent keys. One classical, one post quantum. An encrypted backup downloads before the account is deployed. Your password unlocks that backup only on your device. Keep both safe. There is no recovery service.

Magen deploys your wallet contract and deletes its setup key in the same transaction. No access keys remain. Only the contract moves your funds. Both signatures bind your account, network, nonce, action and expiry. A changed recipient, forged half or replay is rejected on chain.

You can receive NEAR, save contacts and inspect every recorded receipt. There are no invented balances or price charts. This balance comes directly from NEAR. Rejected attempts stay visible too.

Then there is the Q Day switch. It keeps the account, but changes what controls it. Both signatures authorize adding your post quantum key as a native full access key. This is a deliberate change to quantum only control. That key can now act without the classical half, including changing the wallet contract.
